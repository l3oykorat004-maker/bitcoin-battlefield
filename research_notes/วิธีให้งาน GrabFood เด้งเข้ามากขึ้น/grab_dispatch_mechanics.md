## KQ1. What Grab has published about the matching/allocation engine for GrabFood/GrabExpress: objective, batching logic, ETA models, supply–demand balancing

### Takeaway
Grab describes a single AI allocation engine shared by rides and deliveries that (a) draws a candidate pool of online partners inside an "allocation radius", (b) narrows it by ETA computed on the road network, then (c) scores "over 40 real-time factors" (ETA, driver trip preferences, fraud prevention, vehicle suitability, regional traffic laws, order batching), with the stated objective of "the fastest, most reliable overall match rather than just proximity" plus marketplace "fairness" and efficiency. For food, a batching layer ("hyper-batching", in Thailand since 2020) groups 2+ orders per trip based on item type/weight, prep time, partner vehicle type, merchant wait time, traffic and weather; Grab reports batched orders cost users 8% less and paid drivers 5% more per transit hour (Q3 2023), and that 6 in 10 Saver orders were batched (Q3 2024).

### Cited Findings

- Factor list: the platform "uses an AI algorithm that evaluates over 40 real-time factors" and "prioritises estimated arrival time, driver trip preferences, fraud prevention, vehicle suitability, regional traffic laws, and order batching", "ensuring the fastest, most reliable overall match rather than just proximity" — [Inside Grab (SG mirror)](https://www.grab.com/sg/inside-grab/stories/matching-driver-partners-and-riders-allocation/)

### Inferences
- The allocation is a two-stage funnel: a geographic/ETA pre-filter, then a multi-factor score. A rider who is marginally farther but has a shorter road-network ETA, is on auto-accept, has a strong completion history, or completes a batch route efficiently can beat a nearer rider.
- Because the batcher works from "deterministic time estimates" and merchant wait time, riders who make the system's predicted times reliable (fast arrive/pickup taps, low cancellation) make themselves cheaper for the optimiser to assign in batches.
- Grab's revenue logic (lower fee per order, higher earnings per transit hour) makes Saver/batched orders a growing share of 2W supply usage; opting out of or cancelling batched legs likely works against a rider's allocation score.

## KQ2. Why Grab says it does not always assign the nearest driver, and what is weighed instead

### Takeaway
Grab's official answer is that straight-line proximity is a poor proxy for ETA and reliability: it ranks candidates by road-network ETA and then by a 40+ factor score covering driver trip preferences (e.g., destination filter), fraud risk, vehicle suitability, local traffic rules, and whether the order can be batched, and it deliberately prioritises auto-accept partners and high-completion partners to avoid re-allocation delays.

### Cited Findings
- Headline position: "Grab doesn't always assign the nearest driver to you—here's why"; the system "first builds a list of nearby drivers and then narrows it by estimated arrival time" and weighs "over 40 real-time factors" — [Inside Grab](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/matching-driver-partners-and-riders-allocation/)
- Road vs straight-line distance: "the driver with the shortest straight-line distance may not be the optimal driver as it could reach the pick-up point with a longer routing distance due to detour" — [engineering.grab.com: Pharos](https://engineering.grab.com/pharos-searching-nearby-drivers-on-road-network-at-scale)
- Cherry-picking cost: "a rejected nearby request can force assigning a farther partner, which raises everyone's wait and lowers efficiency"; Grab "prioritizes allocation to partners who enable auto-acceptance of requests, which reduces the chance that partners cherry-pick only the requests they want to accept" — [Grab public policy blog](https://www.grab.com/sg/blog/public-policy/how-does-grab-match-trips-and-orders-to-driver-and-delivery-partners/)
- Route familiarity: partners "who show more familiarity with a city's one-way streets or motorcycle-only roads may be prioritized for trips that pass through those areas, to cut unnecessary delays" — [Grab public policy blog](https://www.grab.com/sg/blog/public-policy/how-does-grab-match-trips-and-orders-to-driver-and-delivery-partners/)
- Best-suited partner, vehicle: Grab "tries to match the best-suited partner, with vehicle size and type considered for each order" — [Grab public policy blog](https://www.grab.com/sg/blog/public-policy/how-does-grab-match-trips-and-orders-to-driver-and-delivery-partners/)
- Over-supply case: "This happens when there are many partners available in the vicinity, but not enough requests, such as in quieter locations or during non-peak periods" — the algorithm then picks "using its principles and other data", and Grab added a feature matching drivers heading to a preferred destination — [Inside Grab: marketplace principles](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/principles-that-shape-the-design-of-our-marketplace/)
- Thai operational history: in Aug 2020 Grab Thailand's zone-based dispatch split across Bangkok/Nonthaburi/Pathum Thani caused allocation problems; Grab said technical limitations arose ("เกิดข้อจำกัดในเชิงเทคนิคบางประการ") and it would scrap the zoning from 17 Aug 2020 — [Thairath, Aug 2020](https://www.thairath.co.th/news/business/market-business/1907607); background on the 10 Aug 2020 GrabBike protest — [Brand Buffet](https://www.brandbuffet.in.th/2020/08/grab-driver-partner/); Grab's 3-point statement — [Marketing Oops](https://www.marketingoops.com/?p=255664)
- Thai 2022 dispute over a "job booking" system (ระบบจองงาน): riders petitioned Grab Thailand to cancel the booking system; Grab said it would consult riders and conclude within 14 days (3 Nov 2022) — [Thai PBS](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/news/content/321149); [InfoQuest](https://www.infoquest.co.th/?p=247720); [Thai PBS Decode, 23 Oct 2022](https://www.thaipbs.or.th/decode/20221023-riders-protest)

### Inferences
- For a Korat 2W rider the practical levers implied by Grab's own factor list are: being on the road network side of the merchant cluster (short routing ETA, not just straight-line), auto-accept on, destination filter off, both Food and Express/Mart service types on, and a clean fraud/GPS profile.

### Gaps
- Grab does not disclose factor weights, or how often a non-nearest partner is chosen.
- Whether Thailand currently runs any zone or booking-slot layer on top of the national engine (post-2022) could not be verified.

## KQ3. How the heat map, hotspot/AI ride guidance, auto-accept and back-to-back features work; rollout countries/years

### Takeaway
The driver-app heat map is a 5-minute-refresh demand overlay (shades of red; colour ≠ fare; reflects only the service types you have switched on). "AI ride guidance" (GrabRideGuide) layers a predictive model (real-time + historical demand, time of day, weather, events) on top and suggests positioning routes; Grab reports a 21% earnings-per-online-hour uplift in beta (80,000+ drivers, 14 cities) and 250,000+ weekly users by 2025. Auto-accept confers allocation priority; Back-to-Back jobs are offered only while auto-accept is on and the next pickup is within ~5–10 minutes of the current drop-off.

- Heat map semantics (MY): "Passenger demand is now represented by different shades of red. The darker the area, the more demand there!"; "The 'redness' of an area DOES NOT represent the fare" — [Grab MY driver blog: Improved Demand Heat Map](https://grab.com/my/blog/driver/improved-heat-map)
- Heat map legend (PH): "Red = Very High Demand / Yellow = High Demand / Green = Medium Demand" — [Grab PH app features: heat map](https://www.grab.com/ph/appfeatures/heat-map/)
- Refresh and scope (PH): "Demand on the Heat Maps changes every five minutes, so there's no certainty of getting bookings"; "The Heat Map shows a combined demand for the different service types you have enabled" — [Grab PH driver blog: improved demand heat maps](https://www.grab.com/ph/blog/driver/improved-demand-heat-maps/)

## KQ4. Does Grab state that acceptance rate, cancellation rate, completion rate, ratings or GrabBenefits tier affect job allocation or visibility?

### Takeaway
Yes, in general terms: Grab's marketplace-principles page says it gives "priority allocation" to partners "with good reviews and a high trip completion rate" and to those with auto-accept on; its 2018 Driver Timeout feature temporarily stops job offers to partners who "consistently ignore or cancel" bookings; a Philippine page says the completion rate "counts ignored jobs and cancellations". Grab does not publish numeric allocation thresholds; the hard numbers it publishes (e.g., 90% acceptance / ≤10% cancellation per shift in SG; ≥80% / ≤5% in PH 2018) are incentive eligibility rules, not dispatch cut-offs. No source says GrabBenefits tier buys job priority.

### Cited Findings
- Priority allocation wording: "We also want to incentivise high service levels, by rewarding partners who show a strong track record with good reviews and a high trip completion rate. We offer priority allocation to minimise their time driving around looking for a booking." — [Inside Grab: principles that shape the design of our marketplace](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/principles-that-shape-the-design-of-our-marketplace/)
- Auto-accept wording: priority for partners who keep auto-accept on "is done to prevent rider disappointment and unnecessary delays in the system that may arise from partners who cherry pick bookings and turn down requests" — [Inside Grab: marketplace principles](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/principles-that-shape-the-design-of-our-marketplace/)
- Ratings: "high performers, such as partners with higher passenger ratings, may get priority allocation", citing Singapore's "Emerald Circle" scheme — [Grab public policy blog](https://www.grab.com/sg/blog/public-policy/how-does-grab-match-trips-and-orders-to-driver-and-delivery-partners/)
- Going offline is not penalised: "The algorithms do not penalize partners who need to take a break in any way to rest and recharge. Partners are free to turn off the app when they want to, and can resume whenever they are ready." — [Inside Grab: marketplace principles](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/principles-that-shape-the-design-of-our-marketplace/)

## KQ5. Merchant-prep-time-aware dispatch / "order-ready" timing and when a rider is assigned

### Takeaway
Grab publicly describes a "just-in-time" dispatch: a prep-time buffer is inserted before a rider is allocated so the food is ready on arrival; smaller orders get a shorter buffer (assigned sooner), larger orders a longer one; prep-time predictions are learned per merchant from riders' arrive/pickup taps and, in a 2022 Singapore pilot, from Bluetooth beacons at pickup counters.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanism: the system "calculates the 'just-in-time' window from prep time, the time to assign an order to a driver, and the driver's travel time to the restaurant"; "Previously, F&B merchants would only begin food preparation once a delivery-partner had been assigned to the order."; "The buffer time before allocation, which is calculated based on food preparation time, will give merchants the headstart that they need to prepare orders. Smaller orders that require less time to prepare will see a shorter buffer time before a delivery-partner is assigned." — [Inside Grab: cutting down wait times for delivery drivers](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/cutting-down-wait-times-delivery-drivers/)
- Prep-time learning: "Grab's machine learning engine learns to predict a merchant's food preparation time"; "Better estimation of the time needed for a dish to be ready helps the system time a delivery partner's arrival, cutting down on waiting time for the driver-partner." — [Inside Grab: Bluetooth devices predicting prep time](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/these-little-bluetooth-devices-are-helping-grab-predict-food-preparation-time/)
- Signal source: the Grab Driver app asks drivers to tap on arrival and shows a prominent button to confirm pickup once food is ready; manual taps "were sometimes imprecise", so Grab ran a two-month mid-2022 pilot with "35 merchants across three Singapore malls, plus two GrabKitchens", placing beacons "under or beside the counter where drivers collect orders" — [Inside Grab: Bluetooth devices](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/these-little-bluetooth-devices-are-helping-grab-predict-food-preparation-time/)
- Batch routing uses merchant wait time: routing considers "the time delivery-partners need to wait at the merchants" — [Inside Grab: Saver option](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/how-the-saver-option-for-food-deliveries-works/)
- Related patents (assignee not verified in excerpts): "Depot dispatch protocol for aggregating on-demand deliveries" ([US 11,568,355](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11568355), [US 10,872,305](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10872305)) and "System and method for dynamic pairing function optimization" ([US 10,810,536](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10810536)) describe a neural network tracking "the average time for a particular merchant to prepare a particular item" and using predicted ETAs to assign orders to couriers; a separate filing "Reinforcement learning model optimizing arrival time for on-demand delivery services" — [US 2022/0207478 (Justia)](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20220207478)

### Inferences
- Because dispatch is delayed until roughly (prep time − rider travel time), riders positioned at the travel-time radius of busy merchants, not at their doorstep, are the ones whose ETA fits the just-in-time window; riders who log accurate arrive/pickup taps improve the merchant's prep estimate and hence future timing.

### Gaps
- No published figures on how much the buffer cut rider waiting time, nor the beacon pilot's measured accuracy gain, were found.
- Whether the just-in-time buffer and beacon programme run in Thailand is not stated.
- Patent assignees could not be confirmed (fetch blocked); treat as "Grab-associated, unverified".

## KQ6. Anti-fraud / GPS-spoofing detection that can reduce job flow

### Takeaway
Grab lists "fraud prevention" among its allocation factors and describes GrabDefence models that flag fake-GPS use by comparing live telemetry, device sensors and cell-tower signals against a user's historical behaviour, feeding a Risk Orchestration Engine that can trigger step-up verification or disable accounts. Published consequences are suspension/ban (and, in Indonesia, lock-out until fake-GPS apps are deleted); Grab has not published that fraud scores silently throttle job flow short of those actions.

### Cited Findings
- Fraud is an allocation input: the matching engine "prioritises … fraud prevention" among its 40+ factors — [Inside Grab: nearest driver article](https://www.grab.com/sg/inside-grab/stories/matching-driver-partners-and-riders-allocation/)
- Detection method: "One such algorithm is to detect users who manipulate their GPS location using fake GPS apps or other spoofing techniques. By comparing real-time signals from the telemetry, device sensors, cell-towers, and so on against historical user behaviour, the algorithm can flag suspicious deviations." — [Inside Grab: GrabDefence](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/grabdefence-anti-fraud-technology-for-southeast-asias-digital-age/)
- Response pipeline: "If GrabDefence's AI models detect anomalies, they trigger actions through its Risk Orchestration Engine. It identifies and flags high-risk activities and from there, takes steps to mitigate—such as, for instance, disabling fraudulent accounts."; biometric verification: "When a login attempt is identified as being high-risk, it then triggers additional verification methods such as facial recognition." — [Inside Grab: GrabDefence](https://www.grab.com/inside-grab/stories/grabdefence-anti-fraud-technology-for-southeast-asias-digital-age/)
- Spoofing pattern and sanction (MY, 2019): "Some drivers may use fake GPS tools and 'rooted' mobile phones … allowing them to change their position arbitrarily without actually moving" (to appear at malls); "Drivers who were detected to have used GPS spoofing tools are banned"; Grab put platform fraud "far below 1%, below industry averages"; a black market in verified accounts exists — [Malay Mail, 17 May 2019](https://www.malaymail.com/news/money/2019/05/17/drivers-use-gps-spoofing-fake-apps-to-defraud-grab-says-ride-sharing-firm/1754081)
- Spoofing definition and sanction (MY, 2020): "GPS spoofing, where drivers use third party illegal GPS tools to artificially pin themselves in a particular location, such as a shopping mall, though they may be physically some distance away"; Grab said it would suspend partners using illegitimate tools/apps, including modified apps used to steal bookings — [Free Malaysia Today, 20 Aug 2020](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2020/08/20/customer-data-safe-says-grab-over-driver-fraud-report); [SoyaCincau, 21 Aug 2020](https://soyacincau.com/2020/08/21/grab-malaysia-e-hailing-hack-modified-app-steal-bookings-rides/)
- Indonesia "Anti Tuyul": "To regain the access to their account, the driver partners must delete all fake GPS applications from their smartphone." — [Tempo](https://en.tempo.co/amp/920445/grab-indonesia-launches-anti-tuyul-feature); Grab ID GrabDefence launch — [Grab ID press](https://www.grab.com/id/en/press/others/grab-luncurkan-grab-defence-perangkat-teknologi-anti-kecurangan-terbaru-untuk-bantu-mitra-atasi-tindak-kecurangan/)
- Philippines 2W fraud re-education lists a "teleporting GPS" pattern (driver appears in two or more distant places at once) — [Grab PH: 2W Fraud Online Reeducation](https://www.grab.com/ph/blog/driver/car/fraud-online-reeducation-2w/); 4W version — [Grab PH](https://www.grab.com/ph/blog/driver/car/fraud-online-reeducation/)
- Back-to-Back misuse is classified as fraud: not completing the first booking before starting the second "is detected and treated as fraud" — [Grab PH: back-to-back (food delivery)](https://www.grab.com/ph/blog/driver/food-delivery/backtoback/)
- Comparative claim: Spire Research study found Grab's fraud rate "only 1/6 of its closest competitors" (Grab-commissioned) — [Integrity Asia on GrabDefence](https://www.integrity-asia.com/blog/combating-evolving-fraud-grab-releases-grab-defence)
- Generic verification weakness noted in patent literature: location "signals used for verification are easy to fake but hard to verify" — [US 2020/0213855 (Justia)](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20200213855)

### Inferences
- Since "fraud prevention" sits inside the allocation score, a device/location risk flag (e.g., rooted phone, mock-location app installed, GPS jumps, two-device logins) plausibly lowers allocation priority before any visible suspension — but Grab has not said this explicitly.
- Riders with legitimately erratic GPS (poor signal, battery-saver location modes) risk the same "suspicious deviation" signal; keeping the official app, high-accuracy location, and a non-rooted device is the only published mitigation.

### Gaps
- No Grab statement found that confirms silent job throttling ("shadow-banning") for fraud suspects; only suspension/ban/step-up verification are documented.
- No Thailand-specific GPS-spoofing enforcement notice was retrievable (Thai help centre blocked; search budget exhausted before the Thai query ran).
- The GrabDefence article's date is not in the excerpt (GrabDefence launched ~2019; the Inside Grab article is later).
