## Key Question 1: Which merchant clusters within ~5 km of Hua Thale have the highest density of delivery-listed restaurants, and what are their peak times?

### Takeaway
The confirmed anchors are: Lotus's Korat 2 (Hua Thale) on ถ.ราชสีมา-โชคชัย (Hwy 224) inside the tambon; the Terminal 21 / Big C junction where Hwy 224 meets Mittraphap Rd (Hwy 2), now served by a new underpass; Central Korat's 65-rai mixed-use complex; and Save One night market on Mittraphap Rd (16:00/17:00 to 22:00/23:00). No source gave restaurant counts per cluster on Grab or LINE MAN, so density must be inferred from the anchor types rather than from platform data.

### Inferences
- Because Hwy 224 runs straight from Hua Thale to the Terminal 21 / Big C junction, and the underpass there is now open, the Lotus Hua Thale → Terminal 21 corridor is the shortest high-value axis for a Hua Thale rider: chain restaurants at both ends, one straight road, no longer a construction bottleneck.
- Save One's 1,000+ stalls and 16:00/17:00–22:00/23:00 hours make it the single densest evening merchant cluster in the city, but it is on Mittraphap west of the old city, i.e. outside the 5 km radius from Hua Thale (distance not verified; see Gaps).
- The MK listing (opens 10:00) and Lotus's food-court formats suggest Lotus Hua Thale's delivery-relevant window starts at 10:00, with the mall food court and chain outlets as the main in-tambon merchant base.
- Street-food stalls in front of รพ.ค่ายสุรนารี form a small but delivery-friendly cluster adjacent to Hua Thale (Nong Phai Lom side).

## Key Question 2: Where do the biggest customer concentrations live or work, and which are within Hua Thale?

### Inferences
- Hua Thale's own customer base is a mix of new detached-house estates (Supalai Prima Villa class), apartment stock around Lotus Korat 2, and the military households of Fort Suranari / Wing 1 on its western edge; the dense student dorm belt (NRRU/RMUTI, ถ.สุรนารายณ์) is outside the tambon to the north.
- The 27 June 2569 RMUTI welcome day implies RMUTI's 1/2569 semester started around late June / early July 2569, which is when student-area demand (ถ.สุรนารายณ์) re-ignites; SUT and NRRU were not confirmed.
- Hospital-driven demand (staff shifts, visitors) is strongest around Bangkok Hospital Ratchasima on Mittraphap and Maharat in the old city; both are outside Hua Thale but reachable along the Hwy 224 → Mittraphap axis.

## Key Question 4: Rainy-season and weather effects in Korat relevant to delivery demand

### Takeaway
Korat's wet season runs May–October; September is the wettest month at roughly 235–248 mm over 19–23 rain days, July–September each bring about 200–270 mm, and October falls to roughly 160–215 mm; the 30-year annual mean for Mueang district is 1,071.5 mm. Sources disagree on exact figures, and no TMD normals table or data on typical afternoon-storm hours was retrieved.

### Cited Findings
- September: 248 mm over 19 rainy days; October: 159 mm over 13 rainy days — [Weather2Travel Nakhon Ratchasima](https://weather2travel.com/thailand/nakhon-ratchasima/june/)
- September ≈ 235 mm with 23 wet days — [Climate-Data.org](https://en.climate-data.org/location/5993/)
- September ≈ 236 mm, October ≈ 214 mm — [KKday](https://www.kkday.com/en-my/destination/th-nakhon-ratchasima)

### Inferences
- Rain-surge days (and thus delivery-demand spikes with fewer active riders) are most frequent in September, then July–August, with October tapering but still capable of extreme single-day events; the dry November–February window gives steadier, more predictable volume.
- With ~19–23 rain days in September, a Hua Thale rider should expect rain on most working days that month and plan for the Hwy 224 / underpass approaches to be slow when wet.
